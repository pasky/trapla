# Noise from several new roads at one house

Research snapshot: 2026-11-16. This note addresses source aggregation, limits and segment accounting for a planning-game model. It is not a statutory acoustic study. **UNVERIFIED** marks points not established from the sources actually inspected. See [09-noise-earthworks-urban.md](09-noise-earthworks-urban.md) §2 and [11-noise-calculation.md](11-noise-calculation.md) §§1–3 for the existing noise model, Czech manual equations and its explicit current limitation (no finite-segment integration or multiple-route aggregation); those are not repeated here.

## Findings for Trapla

- Treat distinct new/changed road sources at a receptor as sound-energy contributions, not as separate independent chances to spend the same limit. Sum their LAeq for the applicable period, then compare with the applicable limit.
- Do **not** put an unchanged existing road's full exposure into a new-road source sum by default. In project studies, unchanged roads can form the existing/background scenario; a development study may also model a future network and report total situation. Keep these as explicit, distinct comparison cases. A project's traffic redistribution or physically altered road is not unchanged background.
- The current regulation has a specific mixed-road rule: where roads have different limits, the **resulting limit** is the limit of the road whose traffic-noise contribution predominates. It does not say to energy-average limits, select the strictest limit, or apply each limit to the same combined total separately.
- In current NV 272/2011 Annex 3 A, column 2 is +10 dB for outdoor protected spaces of ordinary buildings and column 3 is +18 dB. The distinction is by **date/status of the road**, not a simple class-I versus local-road split. For the ordinary protected outdoor space, with base 50 dB and night −10 dB: column 2 yields 60/50 day/night; column 3 yields 68/58. Under the column-2 rule, class I versus local road alone does not create different limits. If legally distinct road categories/conditions do produce different limits, use the prevailing-contribution rule above.
- Splitting one physical road into editor pieces must not create a +3 dB (or other) artificial increase. Piece contributions can be summed only when each piece represents its actual independent source extent/traffic; do not give each segment the full road's traffic and then add them as separate full-strength sources.
- A barrier serving multiple projects is technically possible, but the inspected materials do not establish a general Czech rule allocating its cost between investors. **UNVERIFIED:** ownership/cost-sharing practice; model it as a project agreement, not a legal default.

## Legal rule: NV 272/2011

[Consolidated NV 272/2011 text](https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/2011-272), §12(1), says road-traffic LAeq,T is determined “pro celou denní (LAeq,16h) a celou noční dobu (LAeq,8h).” §12(3) sets the limit as the 50 dB base plus the applicable Annex 3 A corrections. Annex 3 A explicitly says: “Korekce uvedené v tabulce se nesčítají.” It then states: “Jde-li o souběh pozemních komunikací s různými hygienickými limity hluku, výsledný limit hluku se stanoví podle té komunikace, ze které je příspěvek hluku z dopravy na této komunikaci převažující.” This is a legal **limit-selection rule**, not a separate instruction to combine the two roads' calculated levels. The resulting sound level at the receiver remains a physical energy sum when assessing concurrent sound from roads.

The table's ordinary-building row has 0 / +10 / +18 dB in columns 1/2/3. Its notes say column 2 applies to road/rail transport on roads “umístěny a povoleny … po 31. prosinci 2000”; column 3 applies to roads placed/permitted before 1 January 2001, and specified subsequent maintenance/reconstruction/modernisation/capacity changes. Thus “old noise burden” (stará hluková zátěž) is not a general allowance for existing roads: the higher correction has a defined road-history and activity scope. A newly built road does not get the old-road correction. Whether a relocated existing road is legally a new road or falls within a specific statutory treatment depends on its approval and scope; do not infer from the word “relocation” alone.

The legal table itself does **not** assign a separate +5/+10 correction merely because one source is a local class III road and another is class I. Older studies may quote the previous Annex wording/tables: for example, the 2019 Herálec study (below, pp.18–19) reproduces a pre-amendment table with a +5 dB class-III/local-III category, +10 for major roads, and +20 old-load column. Do not port that historical table into the current legal rule without checking the applicable transitional/proceeding dates. This is consistent with [11-noise-calculation.md](11-noise-calculation.md) §1, which explains the 2023 changes and selects 60/50 for the model's new ordinary road.

## What project studies do with existing and cumulative noise

**EIA acoustic study example — Herálec (CENIA VYS933).** [Hluková studie, December 2019, 23-page PDF](https://portal.cenia.cz/eiasea/download/RUlBX1ZZUzkzM19wcmlsb2hhRG9rdW1lbnRhY2VET0NfMjA5MzA2NzQxMzU5ODg0MDg3OC5wZGY/VYS933_prilohaDokumentaceDOC_10.pdf), inspected pp.3–6, 10–11, 17–20. This is an agricultural facility / associated traffic example, not a multiple-new-roads EIA, but demonstrates the ordinary acoustic-study method of summing project and measured background. It says (p.19): “Zpracovatelé hlukové studie se rozhodli vyhodnotit hlukovou zátěž součtem příspěvku … s dostupným naměřeným hlukovým pozadím lokality.” It labels table columns “Naměřené pozadí”, “Energetický součet pozadí a příspěvku”; p.20 says levels were “energeticky sečteny s hlukem pozadí”. The notes use the **higher** background reading as a proxy in other receptor points. This supports energy addition and shows background is a separately identified existing-condition quantity; it does not prove all unchanged roads must be included in every road project's legal compliance test.

**EIA cumulative-effects example — II/240 / II/101 D7–D8.** [CENIA acoustic study PDF](https://portal.cenia.cz/eiasea/download/RUlBX1NUQzE5NDVfcHJpbG9oYURva3VtZW50YWNlRE9DXzc4NDU2MjU2MTY5Mjg1ODEyMzEucGRm/STC1945_prilohaDokumentaceDOC_6.pdf), opened via Visit Webpage (the extraction returned lengthy excerpts, including the linked health-impact section). The associated health assessment explicitly describes cumulative treatment across road, rail, aircraft and stationary sound: first convert differing source annoyance descriptors where relevant, “následně je proveden energetický součet hladin hluku”; for road+rail it says “použít prostý energetický součet hladin hluku” (pp.32–33 of the extracted document). It says it uses **total noise burden including railway** for health-effect assessment (p.33). This is an EIA health-effects/cumulative assessment, not a statement that every background source is included in the statutory road-specific limit check. Study examines before/project scenarios and multiple variants, so it illustrates cumulative impact accounting, not the exact legal attribution for two new roads under one permit.

**UNVERIFIED:** No inspected road EIA acoustic-study passage was found that directly states, in one sentence, “all proposed roads are energy-summed and that sum is tested against the NV limit,” nor a directly relevant example of two separate road investors being assessed together at one dwelling. The regulatory mixed-limit sentence above is verified, and the inspected EIA examples establish that energy sums are used for concurrent acoustic contributions/background and cumulative impact assessment. Distinguish that evidence from a stronger universal claim.

## Finite segments: no duplication

[Manuál 2018, revize 2020](https://md.gov.cz/getattachment/Dokumenty/Strategie/Hluk/Manual-2018/Zmeny-v-Manualu-2018/Manual_2018_aktual_2020.pdf.aspx) is the official calculation reference cited in [11-noise-calculation.md](11-noise-calculation.md) §§1–3. Its source/propagation model is used by road-noise studies to calculate contributions from the modeled road geometry and then combine contributions acoustically. The inspected material for this task did not establish an exact manual quotation prescribing road segmentation and summation formula across arbitrary finite road pieces; do not present that specific clause as verified.

For the game, the unambiguous physics and safe implementation rule are:

```text
Ltotal = 10 log10(Σ_i 10^(Li/10))
```

Here each `Li` must already be the contribution from that **actual segment's** traffic, length/geometry, propagation and time period, evaluated at the same receptor. If an editor splits a continuous road but preserves geometry and per-length traffic, its total predicted contribution should be invariant (within numerical tolerance). The existing Trapla model instead treats nearby stations as alternative local sections of an effectively infinite line source; as [11-noise-calculation.md](11-noise-calculation.md) §3 states, “they are not independent full-Q sources to add together.” That is essential to avoid counting whole-road traffic multiple times. Multiple genuinely distinct nearby roads are additive; sampled cross-sections/stations of the same road are not extra sources.

## Barriers and responsibility

The examined acoustic and EIA materials support assessing mitigation against the modeled combined condition, but do not identify a general statutory rule for the owner or apportionment of a common wall when two independent road schemes contribute to the exceedance. In practice a wall can be incorporated in a scheme's design/approval conditions, or coordinated between investors through project agreements, land rights and maintenance arrangements; **the latter is a practical possibility, not established here as a binding Czech rule or sourced example**. No reliable inspected example of a shared wall paid jointly by two road investors was found. Therefore the game can show “shared mitigation needed” as a coordination issue, but should not hard-code a legal payer split.

### Practical rule set

1. Compute each physically distinct road's day and night contribution at the receptor; energy-sum contributions for the combined acoustic level.
2. Select the statutory limit applicable to the receptor and relevant source/road status. If concurrent roads have different limits, apply Annex 3 A's prevailing-contribution road rule to choose the resulting limit; do not average limits.
3. Report existing unchanged roads as existing/background and separately compare existing vs future/project scenarios when relevant. Only add their modeled energy to the combined-condition value if the assessment question is total exposure; do not call the sum “new-road contribution.”
4. Attribute road segments without duplication: splitting one road changes neither total traffic nor source energy. Never sum repeated full-Q samples along one alignment.
5. Keep joint-barrier cost responsibility provisional unless a project-specific agreement/legal allocation is modeled.
