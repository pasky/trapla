# 74 — Running costs of tunnels, bridges and open road; how a Czech CBA counts them (2026-11-17)

Research only. Companion to research/73 (the D35 Dětřichov question). Goal: sourced rates for an *optional*
"running costs over 30 years" line, shown beside the construction bill, so a long tunnel can be weighed against
viaducts or earthworks. Research subagents (research mode) searched; I opened the key sources myself (marked y).
**Derived** = my arithmetic.

## Findings at a glance

- **Per route-metre a twin tunnel costs ≈ 2–4 × as much to run as a viaduct or open road.** Swiss national roads
  (ASTRA research report, 2008) spend 246–286 k CHF per (virtual) km and year on a twin tunnel, against 63.5 k on
  open motorway (per *virtual* km, which counts widenings and junctions; ratios approximate). This covers operational
  maintenance with the energy for lights and fans. Tunnel technology is renewed
  every 15–20 years at ≈ 0.15–0.19 M CZK per tube-m (Panenská + Libouchec 2025; Branisko study 2025).
- **A Czech road CBA has no separate rate for tunnels or bridges.** The ministry method (Rezortní metodika 2017,
  updated 06/2023) prices road upkeep with HDM-4 standards per km of road class. Structures are to be costed
  individually from the owner's data. The method's simplified table (railway chapter) gives bridges and tunnels
  1 % a year plus repairs at ¼, ½ and ¾ of a 60–80-year cycle. These are percentages of a future major repair, not
  of the original price, so they do not carry over directly. Conventions: 30 years, 3 % real social discount rate,
  residual value counted; economic life of road bridges 75 years, tunnels 90.
- **German ABBV (2010)** is the cleanest published table of lives and annual upkeep as % of construction cost:
  - mined tunnel shell 130 years, 0.9 %;
  - tunnel equipment 20 years, 2.0 %;
  - prestressed bridge deck 70 years, 1.3 %; substructure 110 years, 0.5 %;
  - energy excluded.
- **D35 example:** over 30 years the real layout (3.95 km twin tunnel, 440 m of bridges) costs **≈ 0.5–1.3 bn more to
  run** (present value, central 0.9) than Auto's 1.9 km tunnel with 2.3 km of bridges. Running costs favour the shorter tunnel.

## 3. Open road and earthworks

- Czech CBA Tab. 8.39 (HDM-4 standards, VARS Brno, 2017 prices **incl. VAT**): four-lane motorway, asphalt under
  15 years, summer 687,365 + winter 380,308 = **1.07 M CZK per km a year**; over 15 years 1.59 M. Periodic resurfacing
  comes on top (Tab. 8.40: 500 CZK/m² at 8 years, 950 at 16 years, 3,000 at 28 years for the heaviest traffic class).
  Opened (y).
- The pavement exists on bridges and in tunnels too, so in a comparison of layouts it largely cancels. Winter
  service in a tunnel is smaller (not quantified).
- No sourced annual rate for cuttings and embankments as such. ABBV has none; the Czech economic life of the
  "zemní těleso" is 65 years. Leave at zero beyond the road standard.

## 4. How a Czech CBA treats all this

- §8.1.5: road infrastructure running costs come from HDM-4 with a maintenance standard per road class. There are no
  tunnel or bridge rows. Structures go into the individual calculation or the simplified Tab. 8.34 model.
- §5 conventions (2023 update): reference period **30 years**; social discount rate **3 %** real (financial 2 %);
  residual value counted. Economic lives (Tab. 5.12): road bridges 75, tunnels 90, earth body 65, wearing course
  12 (asphalt) / 25 (concrete).
- Road users' costs (VOC, time) come from HDM-4. Its fallback rates (Tab. 8.51, CZK per vehicle-km) do not depend
  on grade. The Slovak ÚHP reviews show "Prevádzkové náklady" as their own CBA line: Turany–Hubová 81.6 M€
  discounted; Branisko 2nd tube 105–113 M€ discounted.

**Presentation:** a separate figure, "Running costs, 30 years: … bn CZK". It is not in the construction bill or the
score unless the owner turns it on. Discounting at 3 % follows the Czech method. This is running costs only: a
full CBA would also count residual value, which differs between layouts (more tunnel shell, more bridge).

For the other side of the scale, road users: Auto's summit is 50 m higher (564 against 514 m), so each vehicle climbs
50 m more in either direction. I/35 today carries ≈ 15,000 vehicles a day, 3,400 of them heavy (ŘSD census 2025,
the pack's traffic.json).

Fuel only, physics estimate, PROVISIONAL:
- a heavy vehicle (20–30 t mean) needs m·g·h ÷ (35.8 MJ/l × 0.40) = 0.7–1.0 l per passage; a car 0.09 l;
- that is ≈ 1.2–1.7 M l a year, at 12–15 CZK/l economic price (Tab. 8.49: diesel 11.68 in 2017);
- **≈ 0.3–0.5 bn** present value over 30 years.

Time lost by trucks on 3.8 % grades and climbing lanes are not estimated (HDM-4 needed). **Low confidence.**
