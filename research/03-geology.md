# Geology / geotechnics as construction-cost evidence

**Scope:** feasibility research, not a design specification. Reviewed 2026-11-13 (licence findings updated in `00-feasibility-map.md`). Map geology is a prior, not a substitute for corridor-scale investigation; engineering properties can vary within a mapped polygon. **UNVERIFIED** means I could not confirm the specific item from a primary-source page/service during this review.

**Probe details:** ArcGIS `?f=pjson` and `/layer/query` requests succeeded on GEOCR50, engineering rajons, boreholes, landslide and undermined-area layers. GEOCR50 returned lithology attributes; IG zoning returned descriptive geotechnical character; borehole layer schema exposed borehole/Quaternary fields; landslide schema exposed activity. Thus service-based feature queries are practical, while WMS alone would only return pixels/limited GetFeatureInfo. Where we did not obtain an intersecting sample, field schema—not point value—is what was verified.

## Translating geology into difficulty (rule-of-thumb, not normative design)

- **Excavation:** Czech road-earthwork practice references ČSN 73 6133 (current classes of excavability I–III); older ČSN 73 3050 used classes 1–7. Standards are not freely redistributable; exact class definitions and quantitative rates **UNVERIFIED**. Internationally, Caterpillar’s [Performance Handbook](https://www.cat.com/en_US/support/operations/tech-tips/earthmoving/rippability.html) uses seismic P-wave velocity and machine-specific rippability curves. A mapped “sandstone” is not enough: cementation, weathering, bedding, jointing and groundwater drive whether excavated, ripped or blasted.
- **Slopes:** common preliminary highway embankment slopes are around 2H:1V; flatter (e.g. 3H:1V) for weak/soft soils, steeper engineered slopes only after stability checks. Soil cut slopes commonly start around 1.5–2H:1V, while competent rock may stand steeper or near-vertical with scaling/bolting. These are broad practice ranges, not code values; **UNVERIFIED** against Czech standards. Rainfall, layering and groundwater can dominate lithology.
- **Material balance:** excavated soil can be unsuitable when organic, highly plastic, wet, contaminated, or oversized; reusable granular soil/rock can reduce haul/import costs. Loose excavation swells and compacted fill shrinks/densifies; factor depends on material (often tens of percent). Concrete factors by lithology **UNVERIFIED**; should be a configurable uncertainty band, not a fixed universal number.
- **Soft ground:** peat, organic soils, alluvial clay and loose saturated deposits can require preload/consolidation, drainage, stabilization, replacement or piles under embankments/structures; settlement duration and treatment can exceed direct excavation cost. Map indicators: Quaternary alluvium, borehole `max_q`, water data and local borehole logs; geological map alone cannot give consolidation parameters.
- **Landslides:** D8 motorway landslide at Dobkovičky (May 2013) is a Czech case of major movement in the České středohoří; [ČGS service](https://mapy.geology.cz/arcgis/rest/services/Geohazardy/svahove_deformace/MapServer) and [D8 technical case search](https://www.google.com/search?q=D8+Dobkovi%C4%8Dky+landslide+2013+geology) are relevant. Flysch of the Carpathian arc has alternating permeable sandstone/weak claystone and documented landslide susceptibility. Exact event geology/cost figures **UNVERIFIED** here. Existing landslide registers are valuable negative/positive evidence but absence is not safety.
- **Karst/mines:** carbonate karst may bring cavities, sinkholes and groundwater conduits; old workings bring collapse/settlement risk. Both are discrete hazards that coarse lithology maps understate.
- **Tunnels:** NATM/NRTM excavation/support classes (or Q/RMR rock-mass systems) are assigned from actual face mapping, groundwater, stress and discontinuities—not surface lithology alone. Poor ground means shorter advances, heavier support, probe grouting and possible face treatment; cost differences can be multiples. **UNVERIFIED:** no defensible universal “good rock vs poor ground” multiplier or standardized Czech NATM class cost table found. Avoid presenting one as an empirical constant.

## Practical reclassification evidence

CGS’s own **1:50k engineering-geological rajons** already reclassify geology into polygons with engineering character and typical rocks; this is a much stronger starting lookup than inventing excavability directly from lithostratigraphic names. REST probe confirms queryable attributes. Use as broad geotechnical zonation, augmented by GEOCR50 lithology, landslide/mine polygons, borehole depth/log indicators, hydrogeology and terrain. A game-facing mapping could label categories (loose granular / cohesive-soft / weathered rock / competent rock / anthropogenic fill) with provisional excavation, slope, reuse, tunnel and foundation-risk bands, explicitly as **heuristics**. No published nationwide deterministic lithology→ČSN class / cost table verified. The only reliable route-specific answer needs geotechnical investigations and engineering design.
