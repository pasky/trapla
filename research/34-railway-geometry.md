# Conventional railway geometry evidence for Czech route design

Research date: 2026-11-15. Scope: new standard-gauge (1,435 mm) conventional line, one or two tracks, mixed passenger/freight, design speed to 160 km/h and a possible 200 km/h option. This is source reconnaissance, not a compliance specification. The existing [research/23-railway-evidence.md](23-railway-evidence.md) remains authoritative for its ballast/formation evidence (SŽDC S3 X), older cant teaching values (VUT 2009), and noise limits. Values below are classified as current TSI, Czech secondary/teaching evidence, or unresolved; no teaching or project value is silently promoted to a current Czech norm.

## Findings by design topic

### 1. Longitudinal gradient

- **No verified Czech current maximum for a new mixed passenger/freight celostátní railway was recovered.** Do not set 12.5‰ as a validated national-line limit from this pass. That number was not located in an accessible authoritative source.
- **TSI INF, Regulation (EU) 1299/2014 consolidated 2023-09-28, §4.2.3.3** does not prescribe one general maximum gradient for all conventional mixed-traffic lines. It says new-line passenger-platform tracks where vehicles are regularly attached/detached and new stabling tracks “shall not be more than 2,5 mm/m”; it also allows **35 mm/m** for main tracks on new **P1 lines dedicated to passenger traffic**, only if the 10-km moving-average profile is ≤25 mm/m and continuous 35 mm/m lengths ≤6 km. Thus 35‰ is explicitly a passenger-only TSI exception, not a mixed-traffic ruling gradient. The TSI’s general criterion is “Maximum gradients for main tracks shall be defined taking into account the characteristics of the traffic intended to operate on the line as well as the reference performance parameters defined in point 4.2.1.” Source: EUR-Lex consolidated INF text, Annex §4.2.3.3, [CELEX:02014R1299-20230928](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02014R1299-20230928). This consolidated text includes the 2023 amendments; no later INF replacement was identified in the preceding evidence note.
- **Older teaching only:** Otto Plášek, VUT FAST, *Úvod do železničních staveb*, PDF page 27, source PDF metadata 2009 (see source list), says: “Největší dovolený podélný sklon na železniční trati nemá přesáhnout 40 ‰ (pokud je sklon větší než 40 ‰, musí být stanoveny podmínky provozování dráhy).” The same page says gradient is established separately for each line with regard to intended traffic, speed, locomotive power and braking. This broad teaching statement is **not** a supported mixed freight/passenger design maximum.
- **Curve reduction / `R/650`: not verified.** No source found in this pass establishes a current Czech formula for reducing grade in horizontal curves, or a tunnel/station reduction. Do not implement `R/650` without a primary standard or project source and a clear definition of units and applicability.

### 2. Vertical curves

- **TSI INF current numerical floor:** Annex §4.2.3.5 states: “The radius of vertical curves (except for humps in marshalling yards) shall be at least 500 m on a crest or 900 m in a hollow.” These are very low interoperability minima, not an appropriate operational design target for a conventional line at 160/200 km/h. TSI §4.2.3.5 has no general gradient-break threshold in the extracted text. Cite the Annex section (not a page) in the EUR-Lex source above.
- **Older Czech teaching evidence:** Plášek, *Úvod do železničních staveb*, PDF p. 28, describes rounding a grade break by a second-degree vertical parabola; extracted notes from this deck give rules on p. 28 and refer to `Rv = 0.4 V²` for a grade break exceptionally placed in a curve of `R < 500 m` (or in a transition at curvature <1/500), with minimum 2,000 m. The slide states minimum rounding radii `Rv,min = 2,000 m` for `V ≥ 80 km/h`; below 80, desired/limit 2,000 m and absolute minimum 1,000 m; radius rounded up to a full 100 m. **The exact general formula/threshold for when a vertical curve is required is not sufficiently clear from the extracted text to assert as a current rule.** Treat these as an older lecture’s reading of its cited sources, not current norm values.
- **No verified general current Czech `Rv = 0.4 V²` rule, minimum 2,000/5,000 m, or threshold gradient difference was found.** TSI minimum radii are not equivalent to the higher Czech design rules suggested by the lecture.

### 3. Horizontal curves, transitions, intermediate tangents

- **TSI INF:** Annex §4.2.3.4 says minimum design horizontal radius “shall not be less than 150 m” for new lines, while requiring selection with regard to local design speed. This is an interoperability lower bound, not a speed-specific conventional-line design radius. TSI §4.2.3.4 requires reverse curves with small radii to prevent buffer locking and points to a separate specification for straight intermediate lengths; it does not set a general `R = 11.8 V²/(D+I)` design allowance or Czech transition-length formula.
- **Czech older teaching (not current):** Plášek, PDF p. 23, distinguishes the then-cited Vyhláška 177/1995 minimum for celostátní dráha: 500 m for new formation, 300 m for reconstruction/modernisation without new formation; and ČSN 73 6360-1:2008 categories `Rlim=300 m`, `Rmin=190 m`. These are generic minimums, not radius-at-speed design rules, and the slide cites an older edition. It does not establish the contemporary formula as a universal constraint.
- **Cant formula warning:** The physics relationship `D_eq ≈ 11.8 V²/R` is not a design-radius permission rule. Inverting it to `R = 11.8 V²/(D+I)` only yields a radius if both selected cant and allowed deficiency are justified for the traffic, vehicles, transition rates, and relevant standard. `research/23-railway-evidence.md` documents VUT 2009 cant/transition values, which remain older teaching values.
- **Transition curve / cant-ramp minima:** No verified current Czech minimum transition length, cant gradient, rate of change of cant or cant deficiency, or applicable minimum straight between curves was found. The VUT 2009 slides (23 note, pp. 11–14) provide historical teaching values; they are not promoted here. TSI cross-reference for reverse curves does not itself give a universally usable length in this research pass.

### 4. Double track and formation

- **TSI INF new lines, standard gauge, speed ≤160 km/h:** §4.2.3.2 gives no general minimum track-centre value at or below 160 km/h in Table 4; distance must be specified for the design and meet the applicable limit installation distance. For new lines in `160 < v ≤ 200 km/h`, Table 4 minimum nominal distance is **3.80 m**. Quote: “The nominal horizontal distance between track centres for new lines shall be specified for the design and shall not be smaller than the values from the Table 4.” These are EU interoperability minima, not necessarily SŽ spatial-clearance/project requirements; aerodynamic margins are stated as considered. [EUR-Lex consolidated TSI INF, Annex §4.2.3.2, Table 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02014R1299-20230928).
- **Older Czech teaching:** Plášek, PDF p. 42, gives open-line track-centre distance 4,000 mm for `V≤160 km/h` in straight and curves `R≥250 m`, recommends retaining 4,100 mm where existing and workable, and for new construction `160<V≤200 km/h` gives **4,200 mm** (4,000 mm may remain in modernisation). The same slide has different station values. This is a 2009 teaching deck and should be checked against current SŽ S11/ČSN 73 6320 before use. The fact TSI’s 3.80 m at >160 and the lecture’s 4.20 m differ reflects their different status/scope; do not substitute the TSI floor for the Czech design clearance rule.
- **Formation section for double track, crossfall, ditches, slope ratios and path:** no verified current general SŽ S4 numeric section was recovered. Current source hierarchy in [research/23](23-railway-evidence.md): SŽ S3 X clause 38/Fig. 4 covers a no-cant **single-track** section only; S3 refers structural-layer requirements to SŽ S4. It cannot be doubled by simply adding a track-centre distance, as widening, track cant, walkway, drainage and formation requirements can change the section. A search result for the official 2025 TKP 6 says earth formation must meet SŽ S4 when ballast rests directly on it, but no double-track dimensional rule was extracted. No sourced 5% formation crossfall, ditch size, 1:1.5 earth slope, or general walkway width is adopted.
- Search results included a publicly reproduced SŽ S4 2024 text on Scribd and student projects that quote a basic 3.1 m half-width. The S4 primary document and its relevant diagrams/tables were not independently retrieved and inspected here; these are not sufficient for a normative number. Do not claim S4 dimensions on that basis.

### 5. Rail bridges, tunnels and road clearances

- **Typical rail bridge/tunnel cross-sections:** no general current SŽ S11/ČSN 73 6320 numerical width or section for single/double track, electrified/unelectrified structures was verified. Track centres, selected clearance gauge, cant, electrification and structural system determine the section. The teaching deck shows figures captioned “Mostní průjezdný průřez jednoduchý/sdružený” (PDF pp. 40–41), but no dependable current dimensional table was recovered. **Unknown.**
- **Rail bridge over a road (vertical clearance under rail bridge):** no general Czech minimum was verified. It depends on road class, road design, any future resurfacing/utility allowances and project approvals. Do not use the example CENIA project dimensions as a general rule.
- **Road bridge over electrified rail (`volná výška`):** no verified current Czech exact minimum (including the suggested ≥6 m) recovered. It must be derived from the governing railway clearance/electrification profile and road-bridge requirements; SŽ S11/ČSN 73 6320 are the indicated clearance sources per [research/23](23-railway-evidence.md). A single “6.0 m” value is not established here.
- **Project evidence is not a generic standard:** CENIA EIA documents contain object-specific free widths/heights. For example, the 2004 *Silnice I/11 – Hrádek – průtah* CENIA notice reports one project’s bridge free width 10.25 m and minimum height under bridge 2.65 m, explicitly for that road/structure; [PDF](https://portal.cenia.cz/eiasea/download/RUlBX01TSzEyN19vem5hbWVuaURPQ18xLnBkZg/MSK127_oznameni.pdf), document date 2004-09-15, search-indexed snippet. This is not a railway-clearance standard and is not adopted as a railway value.

### 6. Level crossings

- **Do not state a blanket rule that all new Czech lines, or all lines above a threshold of 160 km/h, are prohibited by Act 266/1994 from having level crossings.** The current consolidated Act’s §6(1) says: “Pokud se železniční dráha kříží s pozemními komunikacemi v úrovni kolejí, musí být křížení označeno a zabezpečeno.” §6(2) assigns the scope and method decision to the rail administrative authority after the police authority’s prior opinion. The statute establishes marking/protection and decision powers, not a general ban in those paragraphs. [Act 266/1994 Sb., §6, current online consolidated text](https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1994-266).
- **Speed threshold:** Act §8(1)(b) defines a 100 m protective zone from the outer-track axis for a national railway “vybudované pro rychlost větší než 160 km/h”; that is a protective-zone rule, **not** a quoted level-crossing ban. [Same Act, §8(1)(b)].
- **Vyhláška 177/1995 Sb.** §3 identifies level crossings and §4 sets security-device requirements. It says a railway crossing with a road at rail level (“přejezd”) is marked by a warning cross (§3(1)); §4(1) allows warning-cross-only protection in specified low-speed/low-risk cases, while §4(2) requires light level-crossing protection for other crossings. No outright new-line ban above 160 km/h was located in the inspected current text. [Vyhláška 177/1995, §§3–4](https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1995-177).
- **ČSN 73 6380:** its publicly visible scope says it “stanoví požadavky na projektování, stavbu a přestavbu úrovňových křížení pozemních komunikací s dráhami” (sets requirements for design, construction and reconstruction of at-grade road/rail crossings). Public preview: [ČSN preview](http://csnonlinefirmy.agentura-cas.cz/html_nahledy/73/32669/32669_nahled.htm). Full normative text is paywalled/not reviewed. A media article says crossings may exist up to 160 but is not a legal source. **The exact norm/legal clause forbidding a newly built crossing for a line >160 km/h, if any, remains unverified.** For game design, an engineering policy of grade-separated road crossings on a new 160/200 km/h main line may be prudent, but it must be labeled as design policy unless its binding primary source is supplied. Exemptions/definitions for station internal paths, private access or operational crossings need separate treatment.

## 7. Gradient follow-up (2026-11-15)

### Findings

- **No generally applicable numeric maximum for new mixed-traffic Czech celostátní or regionální lines is verified here.** In particular, the often-repeated **12.5‰** is evidenced below as a project/design criterion for a new conventional-link study, not as a blanket ČSN limit for all mixed lines. The 2009 Plášek lecture’s 40‰ sentence remains secondary teaching and is not a normative mixed-traffic limit.
- **ČSN 73 6360-1 clause/value unresolved.** The accessible public catalog preview confirms the standard’s subject, but not its full normative clauses; search results, theses and lecture material inspected did not yield verifiable wording or a table establishing a general maximum by celostátní/regionální line category. The 2013 edition and Z1 were not independently inspected. Do not attribute either 12.5‰ or 35‰ to a verified clause of this standard. [ČSN catalog preview](https://nahledy.normy.biz/n.php?i=511473). The separate VUT thesis result claiming that ČSN sets 35‰ lacks enough accessible context to establish the relevant clause, traffic type, or whether it is quoting the norm accurately.
- **Project-specific evidence for 12.5‰:** a presentation by Radek Čech, *Studie proveditelnosti nového železničního spojení Praha* (published online 2018; slide/page 7 refers to project contract signed 17 March 2017), lists “maximální podélný sklon 12,5 ‰” among the new-link study parameters. This is useful evidence of a design value adopted for that project/study, not proof of a universal national rule; the excerpt does not itself establish freight train performance. [PDF](https://www.ceskainfrastruktura.cz/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Radek-%C4%8Cech-S%C5%BDDC.pdf), p. 7.
- **TSI INF (Regulation 1299/2014, consolidated 28 Sep 2023), Annex §4.2.3.3:** no numeric general maximum is prescribed for mixed-traffic main tracks. It states: “Maximum gradients for main tracks shall be defined taking into account the characteristics of the traffic intended to operate on the line as well as the reference performance parameters defined in point 4.2.1.” §4.2.1 traffic codes F1/F2 and P2/P4 describe reference performance (including axle load/train length and speed) but do not add a gradient cap for those codes. The provision permitting 35 mm/m applies specifically to new main tracks on **P1 lines dedicated to passenger traffic**, subject to profile/length conditions; it is not a mixed passenger/freight allowance. New tracks where passenger trains regularly attach/detach and stabling tracks have the separate 2.5 mm/m ceiling. [EUR-Lex consolidated TSI INF](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02014R1299-20230928), Annex §§4.2.1, 4.2.3.3, Table 3.
- **TEN-T regulations:** neither Regulation (EU) 2024/1679 nor predecessor Regulation (EU) 1315/2013 supplies a 12.5‰ maximum-gradient requirement for new core/extended-network freight lines. Regulation 2024/1679 Article 17(1) and Annex I set rail-infrastructure requirements and dates; Article 17(1)(a) states “full electrification of the railway lines and, to the extent necessary for the operation of electric trains, of the sidings”; the enumerated parameters do not establish a maximum gradient. Regulation 1315/2013 Article 12 and Annex I likewise concern infrastructure requirements, without the proposed 12.5‰ grade cap. These are network-development requirements, not Czech track-geometry standards. [2024/1679, Official Journal text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1679/oj/eng), Article 17 and Annex I; [repealed 1315/2013](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32013R1315), Article 12 and Annex I.
- **SŽ design rules / other projects:** no accessible primary SŽ S3, SM011, “Technické podmínky pro navrhování,” or RS1 technical-specification clause establishing a general mixed-traffic maximum was verified. The 12.5‰ Prague-link study above is project evidence only. The VUT search-result thesis saying “ČSN 73 6360-1 … maximální podélný sklon 35” is not adequate to resolve whether that concerns a passenger-only/high-speed case or a misattribution. No clause/page-backed SŽ rule is asserted.
- **Curve/tunnel grade reductions:** no reliable primary source or project clause was located for “redukce sklonu v oblouku,” `R/650`, or a tunnel-specific reduction. **Not found; do not implement as sourced rule.**
- **Vertical curve formula:** no independently verified current ČSN clause/page for `Rv = 0.4·V²` was recovered. The prior note’s Plášek p. 28 is older teaching and refers to a special case; it is not a verified current general rule. TSI INF Annex §4.2.3.5 separately states the minimum vertical-curve radii (crest 500 m, hollow 900 m), but that does not validate the Czech formula.

For a valid automatic-design mode, these findings support only a TSI-minimum reference layer (track centres 3.80 m for new 160–200 km/h lines; vertical curve radii 500 m crest/900 m hollow; horizontal radius floor 150 m) with explicit warning that these are not sufficient Czech mixed-traffic design criteria. The 2009 lecture can inform provisional research/test scenarios only, not hard constraints. **Do not score mixed-line grade, Czech vertical curve validity, speed-specific horizontal radius/transitions, double-track S4 formation/drainage, bridge/tunnel clearance or a categorical level-crossing ban until current Czech primary rules/project assumptions are checked.**
