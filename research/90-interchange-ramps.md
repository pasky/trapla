# Interchange ramps on Czech motorways: geometry, spacing and cost

Research note for the game. Publicly accessible Czech design studies reproduce some ČSN 73 6102 provisions, but do not constitute access to the complete current standard. **High** confidence means a norm table or verbatim reproduction; **medium** means an actual project design/lecture; **low** means secondary or unverified. Numbers below are design evidence, not a substitute for current-standard review.

## 1. Ramp cross-section

A useful detailed source is the VUT thesis *Studie MÚK Mohelnice – jih na dálnici D35*, [public thesis PDF](https://www.vut.cz/www_base/zav_prace_soubor_verejne.php?file_id=202047), pp. 10–14 (web text extraction; project-specific design, citing ČSN 73 6102 table 38 for curve widening). It says (p. 10): **“Větve MÚK jsou navrženy jako jednopruhové s šířkou mezi bezpečnostním zařízením 7,25 m”**, then lists:

| Element | One-way, one-lane ramp | Two-way/two-lane ramp on overpass |
|---|---:|---:|
| Lane(s) | 3.50 m | 2 × 3.50 m |
| Guide strip(s) | 2 × 0.25 m | 2 × 0.25 m |
| Paved shoulder, left / wide side | 2.00 m | 0.25 m |
| Paved shoulder, right / narrow side | 0.25 m | 0.25 m |
| Unpaved edge to stated clear-width limit | 2 × 0.50 m | 2 × 0.50 m |
| Stated free/clear width | 7.25 m | 9.00 m |

The study calls the larger 2.00 m edge **“zpevněná krajnice”**, and 0.25 m strips **“vodící proužek”**. Clear-width arithmetic for the first section is 0.50+2.00+0.25+3.50+0.25+0.25+0.50 = 7.25 m. The 9.00 m section is presented as the two-lane SO126 overpass/ramp category width, not as a universal two-lane ramp cross-section. The same study labels a separate one-way, two-lane branch “šířka mezi bezpečnostním zařízením 9,00 m” (p. 20–22), but the extracted description does not enumerate its edge widths. **Do not merge the differing sections into one purported normative detail.** No category code such as R 7.25 or a ramp category designation was established from this source; the project describes branch widths and design speeds rather than assigning a standard branch category code. Confidence: **medium**, real design with referenced standard, not a verbatim ČSN table.

## 2. Design speed by ramp form

The Mohelnice study (same source, pp. 11–12, 20–21) gives concrete examples: V1 loop/return branch **50 km/h**, V2 **60 km/h**, V3 **60 km/h** in variant A and **70 km/h** in variant B, V4 loop branch **60 km/h** in A and **40 km/h** in B. Variant B calls the interchange “deltovitá”; A combines “kosodélné a deltovité”. The study's variety demonstrates that type alone does not determine one speed.

The thesis [L. Marušák, 2018](https://theses.cz/id/thw4tj/40577_Archive.pdf) search extract states: **“výjezdové části větví křižovatek na dálnicích … návrhovou rychlost nejméně 50 km/h”** (departure portions on motorway branches designed at at least 50 km/h). The surrounding context/table and current applicability could not be verified; it is not safe to generalize this fragment to every ramp form or every part of a branch.

**Direct / semi-direct / indirect-loop speed table and typical diamond ramp speed: not found.** Proposed 40–50 km/h for a compact diamond ramp is explicitly **PROVISIONAL**, inferred only from project examples (40/50 km/h loop/return segments), not a claimed ČSN threshold. Confidence: medium for examples, low for this inference.

## 3. Horizontal and vertical alignment minima

The full ČSN 73 6102 tables/clauses for radius, maximum ascending and descending grade, and crest/sag vertical-curve radii at 30/40/50/60/70/80 km/h were not publicly verified. Consequently **no speed-row values are supplied here**; inventing a complete table from typical foreign standards would be inappropriate. The Mohelnice design reports selected alignment values, for example:

- 50 km/h branches with horizontal radius **95 m** and a sag/crest vertical radius example **1,200 / 2,500 m** (V1, pp. 11–12).
- 60 km/h branch examples with horizontal radius **400 m**; reported vertical curves include **1,200 m sag, 900 m crest** (V3, p. 12).
- 40 km/h loop example with horizontal radius **55 m**, sag radius **3,000 m** (V4, p. 21).
- The design uses grades including **+5.0%** and **−4.5%** on selected 60 km/h branches (pp. 12–13), but these are design values, not demonstrated maxima.

These are exact project-reported examples, not minimum radii or maximum grade limits; curvature widening is explicitly checked against ČSN 73 6102 table 38. **Confidence: medium for the cited project values; no confidence as normative minima.**

## 4. Speed-change lanes on D130

The same VUT thesis pp. 9–10 and 20 reproduces design calculations attributed to ČSN 73 6102 for mainline design speed 130 km/h. Its table below transcribes only the reported examples; Lpo is the deceleration lane total, Lpp the acceleration lane total.

| Mainline / ramp speed | Deceleration: Lv withdrawal + Ld braking = Lpo | Acceleration: Lod separation + Lm maneuver + Lz merge = Lpp |
|---|---:|---:|
| D130 / 50 km/h | 100 + 134 = **234 m** | 30 + 175 + 90 = **295 m** |
| D130 / 60 km/h | 100 + 125 = **225 m** | 30 + 230 + 90 = **350 m** (bridge constraint affects Lm) |
| D130 / 70 km/h | 100 + 96 = **196 m** | **350 m** shown for the paired V2 design, but not a speed-specific table value |

The study describes the V2 acceleration length as uniform “podle tab. 9b ČSN 73 6102”; its variant descriptions report both 295 m and 350 m depending on variant/context. Do not treat this as a complete standard table. A separate D35 example also has V3 deceleration at 70 km/h = 100+96=196m. The source does not provide full grade-adjusted rows, parallel-versus-taper comparison, lane width or taper length as distinct components. It does report the motorway auxiliary lane width as **3.50 m**, guide strip **0.25 m** (p. 9). Thus lane width is medium-confidence project reproduction; table totals are medium-confidence examples; full 120/130 by ramp speed/grade, parallel/taper and separate taper lengths remain **UNVERIFIED**.

## 5. Interchange spacing and ramp-to-ramp separation

Search results found secondary sources quoting **4.0 km** minimum interchange spacing: [thesis excerpt](https://theses.cz/id/w6cw6o/54765.pdf) says a proposed MÚK at about 2.0 km is less than a motorway minimum “která činí 4,0 km”; a [regional study](https://www.kraj-lbc.cz/getFile/case:show/id:472887/2024-03-13%2012:56:29.000000) says ČSN 73 6101 requires **4 km** between interchanges on an extravilan high-speed road. The Mohelnice thesis itself says its **1.803 km** spacing is below the standard minimum (p. 27). Conversely, a 2025 road-design forum result quotes **2.5 km** for divided roads, but is not a primary source. Likely the rule depends on road class/design context and clause/exception; distance measurement endpoints (junction centers, ramps, or influence areas) were not confirmed. Use **4 km as a sourced secondary statement for motorway/high-speed road only**, confidence medium-low; exact current standard/exception remains to verify.

**Odbočení–připojení** spacing between successive individual motorway ramp terminals/auxiliary lanes was not found as a separate numeric minimum. Do not substitute interchange spacing: the Mohelnice design says the MÚK interval is affected by a branch connection and separately identifies ramp lane transitions. It does not define a general minimum.

## 6. Ramp terminals on the crossing road

The Mohelnice study is explicit about possible forms rather than a mandatory terminal form. It joins branches and class-II/other roads at two **at-grade roundabouts**, with the overpass SO126 providing the crossing connection; the study specifies outside diameters **45 m** and describes one-lane entries/exits (pp. 13–17). This is an actual design example, not a minimum or a rule. Neither minimum distance from bridge to a terminal nor minimum distance between two terminals on the crossing road was verified. Standard geometry must leave room for ramp curvature, queues, sight distance, bridge abutments and any speed-change areas; no numerical game cap should be presented as ČSN. Confidence: medium for example; **UNVERIFIED** for minima.

## 7. SFDI 2026 costs and real contract calibration

The official [SFDI SPK CN page](https://sfdi.gov.cz/cenove-databaze/spk-cn/) was visited. It confirms: **“SPK CN (duben 2026)”**, download of the supplied [ZIP](https://sfdi.gov.cz/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/spk-cn-1.zip), and **“Schváleno Centrální komisí MD dne 24.4.2026.”** The page describes these as study-stage road-construction cost norms. However the archive/PDF *Průvodní zpráva* was not extracted during this pass, so the task-supplied claim that D-category road norms include earth-built interchange branches assuming **one MÚK per 7 km** cannot be quoted verbatim or checked against its surrounding qualifications. No per-MÚK branch length, area, or percentage share is verified. Mark all such quantity/share assertions **UNVERIFIED** until the ZIP's accompanying report is read directly.

Likewise **A.4.N = 76,700,900 CZK per MÚK** is reported as a task lead, but its exact scope/description was not independently confirmed from the 2026 workbook/report. Do not state that the amount covers a complete interchange including bridges/ramps unless that definition is extracted. The sum is a study-stage norm, not a bid award.

No 2–4 real Czech awarded contracts were verified in this pass to isolate one new motorway MÚK and its scope. Whole motorway contracts that include an interchange are poor unit-price evidence without separating mainline, bridges, crossing-road works, utilities and land. Thus **ramp price per km/m² and isolated MÚK cost remain unverified**. Avoid turning the A.4.N lump norm into a ramp unit price.
