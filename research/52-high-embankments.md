# High embankments (> 10 m): validity of the standard slope, berms, culverts under high fills

Research snapshot: 2026-11-16. CZK excl. VAT. **Sourced** = the cited document states it (quoted, page
given); **derived** = shown arithmetic; **PROVISIONAL / UNVERIFIED** = game assumption or not found.
PDF pages are the PDF page index (not the printed number) unless "printed p." is written.

Question: the engine uses the VL 1 (2022) sheet 32-01 broken fill profile (0–3 m 1:2.5; on fills > 6 m
3–6 m 1:1.75, above 6 m 1:1.5; no benches) at any height, and a 28 m fill over a stream valley comes out
cheaper than a bridge. Is something missing?

## Findings at a glance

- **The standard fill profile has a stated top: 10 m.** The 1995 MD standard sheet VL 2 302.02 draws the
  "NÁSYP NAD 6 m" profile (1:2.5 / 1:1.75 / 1:1.5) with the height dimension **"V = 6,00 – 10,00"**; the
  ČSN 73 6133:1998 teaching summary lists the graded standard solution as "1:1,5 – 6,0 m až **10,0 m**".
  The 2022 VL 1 sheet 32-01 drops the number but keeps note 1 ("při návrhu násypů (zejména vysokých) …
  provést výpočet stability"). Nothing in any Czech source extends the standard profile above 10 m, and
  Kaun & Lehovec / the ČSN 1998 slides say standard solutions are applied (beyond study stage) only "do
  6 m" in simple ground. **Above 10 m a soil fill is an individual (calculated) design.**
- **No Czech or German rule "berm every x m of fill height" exists in the sources found.** Czech standard
  berms on fills are *counterweight* berms (přitěžovací/zatěžovací lavice), VL 2 321.03: width **min. 3.5 m**
  (research/48 said 2.5 m — corrected), top at **(0.25–0.45) H** above the toe, outer slope **1:2**, 8–10 %
  outfall, "na málo únosném podloží v odůvodněných případech". German guidance (Adam, Grundbau-Taschenbuch
  ch. 2.1, Tab. 4) instead **flattens the slope above 10 m**: coarse soils 1:1.5–1.8, silty/clayey sands
  and gravels **1:1.8–2 at 8–15 m**, plastic silts/clays 1:1.5–1.8 at 8–15 m. RAA regular slope 1:1.5 for
  h ≥ 2 m "muss erdstatischen Anforderungen genügen"; berms "bei hohen Böschungen … zweckmäßig oder …
  erforderlich" (no number). 2–3 m berms are documented only for **cuts > 15 m**.
- **Rock-fill is the exception:** VL 1 32-01 note 2 / VL 2 302.02: rock fill may be **1:1.5 above 3 m
  "bez ohledu na výšku"**.
- **Real Czech/Slovak high fills:** D47 Ostrava (stavba 4708) **12 m at 1:2.5 / 1:1.5**, no berm; D3 0310/II
  **12 m** fill with a buried RC frame (3.5 m span, **73.6 m** long); D35 Časy–Ostrov abutment fills
  **up to 14 m** needing consolidation surcharges and staged bridge construction; D1 Mořice–Kojetín
  **15 m** fill built of polystyrene blocks because a soil fill would need "jinak nutné přitěžovací lavice";
  Vranča–Hrdinka **17 m** reinforced fill (highest reinforced in ČR, 2003); D1 Fričovice–Svinia (SK) **22 m**
  reinforced fill with gabion toe and micropiles/soil exchange; Reichsautobahn (1939–42) Bačov **≈ 28 m**
  and Ostopovice **32 m** ("nejvyšší dálniční násyp v ČR"), streams passing under them in **buried
  parabolic vaults (112 m long at Bačov)**. Settlement to 95 % consolidation: 259–1008 days (thesis data).
- **Culverts: standard precast products stop at ≈ 4–7 m of cover (≈ 10–11 m individually).** ŽPSV road
  pipes in fill: DN 800 ≤ 5.9 m, DN 1000 ≤ 4.8 m, DN 1200 ≤ 3.9 m; IZM frames 0.4–**7.0 m** standard,
  **11.0 m** with an individually designed reinforcement; TP 232: precast types "maximální hodnota … cca
  **8,00 až 10,00 m**". Higher covers → a **buried bridge** (přesypaný most), which ŘSD builds (D3 0310/II
  SO 204/206/207; D1 Mořice–Kojetín) and SPK CN 2026 prices at **48,235.18 CZK/m² of plan area** (row
  A.2.P.N), plan area = "součin půdorysné délky nosné konstrukce a šířky nosné konstrukce ve vrcholu"; the
  road over it is **still billed** ("Od délky komunikace se neodečítá délka mostů přesypaných").
- **Answer to the direct question:** VL 1 1:1.5 without berms at 20–30 m is **not** a standard solution for
  soil fills; it is defensible only for rock-fill or as the outcome of an individual stability design on
  good ground. A 28 m soil fill needs a flatter slope (≈ +18–32 % side-slope volume) and, over a stream, a
  buried bridge of ≈ 120 m length instead of a pipe (≈ 4× the pipe price; derived below).
