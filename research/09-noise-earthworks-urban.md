## 2. Noise distance / limits

The legal framework is Government Regulation 272/2011 Sb., consolidated text: [e-Sbírka](https://www.e-sbirka.cz/sb/2011/272). A 2019 CENIA acoustic study expressly applies 60 dB day / 50 dB night: [study PDF](https://portal.cenia.cz/eiasea/download/RUlBX1ZZUzkyMl9wcmlsbGhaRG9rdW1lbnRhdG9jXzYyOTUyOTUyMzcwMTc4NjEyMDg5?x=1) (search-indexed as VYS922, 31 Oct 2019). Applicable limit depends on source/category/existing-road status and the regulation's corrections; the model's 60/50 should be labelled its chosen “main road” rule, not a universal rule for every new road.

Czech examples include the I/23 Třebíč southwest bypass acoustic report (13 Jan 2018: [PDF](https://www.trebicobcanum.net/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/hlukova-studie.pdf)) and I/36 Sezemice bypass study ([PDF](http://www.poklidnepocaply.cz/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/H6-Hlukova-studie.pdf)). These assess receptor locations and mitigation, but their traffic, terrain, pavement, speeds and barriers differ; no directly comparable contour distance was independently extracted. Thus a specific 100 m compliance boundary is **UNVERIFIED** by those studies.

**Screening rule:** retain the current project heuristic `100 m × (Q/9000)^(2/3) × (v/90)^(5/3)`. It gives 100 m at 9,000/day and 90 km/h and ~82 m at 80 km/h. Confidence low. It is not a propagation calculation: it omits heavy-vehicle share/time distribution, road gradient, ground absorption, screening, façade height, pavement, wind and reflections. For legality/design, use a Czech noise study/model and compute day/night separately. Recommended game range 70–150 m at scenario traffic; treat exceedance as exposure risk rather than a deterministic statutory setback.

## 3. Earthworks: OTSKP and geology

**17180 vs 17110.** Official OTSKP index and annual specifications are distributed by SFDI: [OTSKP](https://sfdi.gov.cz/cenove-databaze/otskp/). The item is named “Uložení sypaniny do násypů z nakupovaných materiálů”; typical BOQs list it as a distinct imported-fill placement item, while 17110 is general placement of excavated material. A publicly indexed 2023 OTSKP BOQ identifies 17180 and its technical-specification section: [Hořovice BOQ, May 2024](https://zakazky.kr-stredocesky.cz/document_161266/55b56f777b5049aea4850cbf4ecd1b7b-sp-horovice-komplet-20230504.pdf). **UNVERIFIED:** exact OTSKP 2026 Part I text and whether its price includes all compaction tests/requirements; do not add 17110 to 17180 by default. The project’s 2026 table (00-feasibility-map) has 17180 at 636 CZK/m³ and 17110 at 90–116 CZK/m³; treating both as additive risks double-counting placing/compaction. Resolve by checking current Part I specification and a tender item’s measurement/scope.

**12273/12283/12293.** 2026 OTSKP project extraction gives class I/II/III at 183/391/653 CZK/m³, respectively (2026 prices, per `research/00-feasibility-map.md`; source index [SFDI](https://sfdi.gov.cz/cenove-databaze/otskp/)). Definitions concern excavability/material and method, not a deterministic depth threshold. The proposal “I to 3 m, II to 8 m, III below” is a crude gameplay depth heuristic only, **not a technical definition**. A 2018 OTSKP change document references technical specifications but is not itself a geotechnical classification standard: [OTSKP update PDF](http://www.tridniky.cz/PDF/OTSKP_2018_zmeny.pdf).

**Trutnov foothills:** mapped Permian sandstone/conglomerate and Carboniferous bedrock can vary from weathered/fractured, readily excavated near surface to competent rock requiring ripping/blasting; lithology alone cannot specify class. CGS 1:50,000 geology and queryable engineering-geological regions are described in [research/03-geology.md](03-geology.md), including [GEOCR50 service](https://mapy.geology.cz/arcgis/rest/services/Geologie/GEOCR50_mobil/MapServer) and [engineering-geological rajons](https://mapy.geology.cz/arcgis/rest/services/Geohazardy/IG_rajony50/MapServer). **UNVERIFIED:** route-specific typical class shares and weathered-zone depth near Trutnov; no corridor borehole profile or local excavation log obtained. Prefer SFDI SPK's default 75% I / 20% II / 5% III (2026 norm; see `research/00-feasibility-map.md`) as a neutral starting prior, widen uncertainty in mapped rock, and never encode universal 3/8 m class boundaries as geology.

## 4. Urban corridor versus bypass (key calibration)

Public examples do not support a clean, controlled “town route costs N times bypass” estimate: lengths, structures, road cross-section, land and price years differ. The following establish useful bounds, not a matched experiment.

**Variant-comparison evidence:** Třebíč EIA package includes an acoustic study for the proposed I/23 southwest bypass and evaluates affected receptors ([CENIA study, 2018](https://www.trebicobcanum.net/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/hlukova-studie.pdf)); it is not a verified cost comparison against a through-town new alignment. In Náchod, the EIA alternatives and project history identify a bypass selected to remove transit and report demolitions/tunnels ([CENIA I/33 Náchod notice, 30 Jun 2009](https://portal.cenia.cz/eiasea/download/RUlBX0hLSzUxOF9vem5hbWVuaURPQ18xLnBkZg/HKK518_oznameni.pdf)); comparative variant costs were not extracted. **No two matched EIA town-route vs bypass cost comparisons with verified totals found**—do not claim otherwise. Existing-town widening is not equivalent to building a new parallel corridor through built-up blocks.

**What to model:** For base S 9.5 road construction, urbanity alone is not evidence for a fixed multiplier. SFDI SPK's 2026 study norm adds “other objects” of 49.3% urban versus 30.6% rural—only 1.14× on those add-ons, not total cost (method/data in `research/00-feasibility-map.md`). Its stated base S 9.5 rates are 56.9m/km flat/hilly and 68.0m/km mountainous; indirect/additional objects, bridges, tunnels, utilities and land substantially change actual totals. A new urban cut-through can incur buildings, utility diversions, retaining structures, access/staging constraints, noise mitigation and land, but no sourced national demolition/property share is available here.

**Recommended gameplay calibration:** set generic town corridor cost to **1.5×** same-terrain bypass per km, with uncertainty **1.2–2.5×** for a genuinely comparable at-grade, no-major-tunnel corridor. Add explicit parcel/building acquisition and demolition, utilities, barriers/windows, and structures rather than hiding all in multiplier. For dense urban corridors with major structures/tunnels, allow **>3×** as a separate scenario. This is a model judgement (low confidence), not an empirical Czech average. Náchod's 491m/km versus Mošnov's 112m/km (~4.4×) illustrates how tunnels/topography and scope can dominate, but it is emphatically not an urban-vs-rural causal ratio.

## 5. Geometric barrier surrogate (cost model 5)

Sources visited 2026-11-14:
- [ACT Acoustics, Maekawa calculator](https://www.actacoustics.co.uk/sound-calculators/maekawa.html): explicitly gives simplified single-edge diffraction `10 log10(3 + 20N)`, `N = 2δ/λ`, `δ = A + B − direct`; its single-frequency calculator caps at 24 dB and omits ground/thick-barrier effects.
- [Arup Strutt, barrier attenuation](https://strutt.arup.com/help/Environmental_Noise/EnviroBarrierAtten.htm): distinguishes shadow and illuminated zones, defines the Fresnel number/path difference, and documents more complete Maekawa/ISO/CORTN alternatives. It is not the source of a universal broadband correction.
- [FHWA Noise Barrier Design Handbook, §3.5](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/environment/noise/noise_barriers/design_construction/design/design03.cfm): about 5 dB when line of sight is just blocked; 10 dB attainable, 15 dB very difficult. Insertion loss includes loss of some soft-ground attenuation; finite barrier ends can degrade benefit by up to 5 dB. These are traffic-barrier guidance, not a Czech regulatory method.

**Implemented, PROVISIONAL:** 500 Hz (`λ = 0.68 m`), source 0.5 m above grade / 1.75 m towards the receiver from the road axis, wall at `crown/2 + 1.5 m` based at road grade. On embankments this is the shoulder-side allowance, not the slope toe; bridges use the same simple placement. Top occupied receiver remains `building ground + 1.5 + 3 × (levels − 1)` m. At each station/building pair, its radial protected-façade distance supplies the horizontal coordinate of an equivalent 2D section; no full oblique-ray or line-source integration.

The sightline must be blocked: the geometric sum `A + B − direct` is nonnegative even for an illuminated receiver, so it cannot establish shadow by itself. Give zero benefit when the ray sees over the wall or the receiver lies on the road side of it. In shadow, use `IL = clamp(10 log10(3 + 40δ/λ) − 5, 0, 13)` dB. The **5 dB deduction is an invented conservative allowance**, not a validated conversion from point-source/single-band to distributed broadband traffic: it reserves headroom for omitted source, spectral, ground and end effects. It yields about 11 dB for a 3 m wall protecting a low receiver 30 m from the axis and about 9 dB for a fourth-floor receiver 80 m away. The **13 dB cap** lies between FHWA's attainable 10 and very-difficult 15, avoiding theoretical octave-band gains of 20–24 dB. Near grazing, the deduction gives less than FHWA's typical 5 dB; deliberately conservative, not an assertion of a physical universal. Full Czech noise modelling remains necessary.

This geometric shadow replaces the physically inappropriate distance-independent “−2 dB per metre above the wall” rule. A fourth-floor façade 80 m away can be screened by a roadside wall; a close high receiver may see over even a 5 m wall. None of these choices was tuned to a route-cost ratio.
