# Working strip beside houses: Czech road works

**Research status (2026-11-16).** Checked Czech statutory text, publicly accessible technical material, project documentation search results, and selected overseas guidance. The evidence does **not** establish one universal construction-strip width. Confidence is stated for each conclusion; where a primary source or exact measurement was not verified, this is marked **UNVERIFIED**.

## 1. Statutory road-land boundary

The governing source is [Act No. 13/1997 Sb., § 11](https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1997-13) (officially consolidated text also surfaced through [MZe](https://mze.gov.cz/public/portal/mze/legislativa/ostatni/zakon-1997-13-pozemni-komunikace)). § 11(1) says: “**Silničním pozemkem se rozumí pozemky, na nichž je umístěno těleso dálnice, silnice a místní komunikace a silniční pomocný pozemek.**” § 11(2) describes the road body’s boundary by physical features; search-indexed statutory wording includes: “**vnější okraje zaoblených hran zářezů či zaoblených pat náspů, vnější hrany ...**” (the remainder enumerates roadside structures/features).

**Important correction:** the often-repeated 1 m should not be treated as a blanket *construction* strip or as a universal 1 m extension beyond every earthwork toe. The Act distinguishes the road body from *silniční pomocný pozemek*; that auxiliary land is not a fixed-width statutory safety buffer. § 11 defines the land/legal boundary, not land needed temporarily by a contractor. The [Implementing Decree 104/1997 Sb.](https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1997-104) does not establish a general 1 m construction margin; the checked provisions point to technical standards for particular design matters (e.g. § 34 on retaining walls). **Confidence: high** on the distinction/no universal temporary strip; **medium** on the precise boundary detail, as the accessible web rendering/search extracts did not expose all of § 11(2) continuously. Cite § 11(2) directly in implementation before encoding a precise boundary formula.

## 2. Temporary construction take in Czech practice

No general Czech rule or reliably verified standard prescribing a fixed number of metres beyond the permanent take for all road earthworks was found. Project documents define temporary take according to site, construction method, access, storage and environmental/land constraints; the measured “working strip” can therefore mean something different in each project. Temporary construction land is not equivalent to the land needed to safeguard the house after completion.

Evidence checked:

- [ŘSD document, PPK-PLO 06/22 (PDF)](https://www.rsd.cz/documents/38144/57469/PPK_PLO_06-22.pdf): states “**Hranice silničního pozemku ... je schválena budoucím majetkovým správcem a zakreslena ...**” (project-specific approval/delineation), not a prescribed offset for temporary works. **Confidence: high** for absence of a universal PPK-PLO strip in this document.
- A search-indexed small-project technical report [Obec Zdiby (PDF)](https://obeczdiby.cz/assets/File.ashx?id_org=19241&id_dokumenty=1638) specifies “**Bude se jednat o dočasný zábor o šířce 4,0m.**” This is one project, not a highway-wide standard. **Confidence: medium** (document is identifiable; quote retrieved in search extract, not independently confirmed in full PDF).
- A separate [Neratovice technical report](https://www.neratovice.cz/file/1009335) search extract says “**Pracovní pruh bude vyhrazen v šíři 4,0 – 6,0 m**” and also mentions a temporary take 4.5 m wide. The context is local utility/works documentation, not a general road-earthworks benchmark. **Confidence: medium**.
- A small-road construction document [Březová (PDF)](https://www.brezova-be.cz/wrs_frontend/download.php?file=b-souhrnna-technicka-zprava-k230066263.pdf&param=167998) says temporary take reaches “**od hrany výkopu 2,0m**” in its specified situation. This illustrates that a project can define a narrow, task-specific safety/working margin, not that 2 m is generally sufficient. **Confidence: medium**.
- The [EIA documentation for I/38 Havlíčkův Brod](https://portal.cenia.cz/eiasea/download/RUlBX1ZZUzA3OF9vem5hbWVuaURPQ18xLnBkZg/VYS078_oznameni.pdf) describes temporary take for a site facility and “**pracovní pruh pro pojezd mechanismů, deponie zeminy ad.**” but the search extract gives no strip width. **Confidence: medium** for the qualitative point; no quantitative inference.

Thus, the observed 4–6 m values are **examples**, not a source for a blanket rule, and no verified paired dataset distinguishes motorway/class-I from local-road earthworks. **UNVERIFIED:** a standard “3 m” or “5 m” permanent/temporary offset applicable across Czech road classes.

## 3. Building distance / excavation safety

No general minimum horizontal distance from a road toe, retaining wall, or pavement edge to an existing house was identified in the public sources checked. The [ČSN 73 6101 catalogue record](http://seznamcsn.agentura-cas.cz/Detailnormy.aspx?k=69709) identifies the road-design standard, but its full normative clauses are not freely available there. [ČSN 73 6110 public PDF](https://unmz.gov.cz/files/normalizace/%C4%8CSN%2073%206110/74506.pdf) includes design-specific setbacks (e.g. the search extract notes embankment toe in relation to a bank), not a universal house clearance. [Decree 104/1997 § 34](https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/1997-104) refers retaining walls securing road-body stability to ČSN 73 6201; this is structural design, not a house-demolition radius.

Distances for excavations near foundations depend on excavation depth/geometry, soil, groundwater, foundation depth and construction sequence; they require geotechnical/structural assessment rather than a constant road setback. **UNVERIFIED:** numerical Czech minimum separation from a house for an ordinary road embankment, cut, or wall; numerical vibration/demolition exclusion distance; any claim that ČSN 73 6101/6110 imposes a universal house setback. Do not encode one as a norm without checking the licensed current standards and project geotechnical assumptions. **Confidence: medium-high** that a universal fixed house distance was not established by the checked sources, not proof that no project-specific or specialist provision exists.

## 4. International comparison

- [California DOT, Preliminary Engineering Right of Way FAQ (PDF)](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/env/orw/faq-perw-20230926.pdf) explains temporary easements as project-specific use “**when you need to use land temporarily for construction access, such as for the construction of sidewalks, retaining walls**.” It provides no standard width. This supports the project-specific approach, not a numerical comparison. **Confidence: high** for the quoted general principle.
- [Leicestershire Highway Design Guide, Earthworks](https://www.leicestershirehighwaydesignguide.uk/materials-and-construction/earthworks) says earthworks must comply with MCHW Series 600 and location must be approved; it gives no universal extra width.
- A search for German RAS-Q/RE “Arbeitsraum/Baustreifen” did not verify an authoritative numeric rule suitable for comparison. **UNVERIFIED:** German standard widths and UK/US standard widths. Avoid importing figures from non-authoritative summaries or unrelated utility corridors.

## Recommendation for the game

**Recommended single default for the game: 3 m beyond the actual permanent earthwork/structure footprint, marked PROVISIONAL—not a Czech statutory or standards requirement.** Use it consistently for demolition/buyout screening beside roadwork-generated earthworks, including side-road grading, overbridge ramps, short reroutes and turning areas; measure from toe/ditch/cut edge or wall footprint as appropriate, not from road centerline. This retains the existing pragmatic 3 m value while making its status explicit and its application consistent.

This 3 m recommendation is an **engineering/gameplay assumption**, not a measured Czech national norm. The sources establish only that land needs and temporary working areas are project-specific; the cited 4–6 m working-zone examples cannot justify a permanent 4–6 m house-clearance rule. Small at-grade track/service-road works plausibly need less temporary contractor space than large motorway earthworks, but that is **reasoned, UNVERIFIED**. Since the game’s rule is specifically a common house-conflict screen rather than a contractor’s temporary site boundary, retain one 3 m default across work types and allow project/geometry-specific risk logic (deep excavation, high embankment, wall, poor ground) to supersede it when supported. A narrower default for small works should wait for more representative Czech project evidence.
