# Conventional railway evidence for M (single through track)

Research date: 2026-11-15. Scope: a narrow prototype for a new or relocated, standard-gauge, conventional single through track, passenger and freight mixed, design speeds no greater than 160 km/h. This is engineering-source reconnaissance, not a compliance specification. Values not directly evidenced below remain unknown/provisional; do not convert them into validity checks without further review.

## Governing sources and applicability

- **SŽDC S3 Part I, Change 4** (effective 1 March 2021, subsequent supplements shown in the public PDF; current public version retrieved): [PDF](https://www.spravazeleznic.cz/documents/50004227/139626480/SZDC_S3_Dil_01_sezm1az4_a_sopr1_20220101.pdf/08f34fa0-aabe-465a-8292-5c05e9a24cea?version=4.0). Articles 45–50 refer design of track geometry to ČSN 73 6360-1/-2; article 50 says spatial arrangement is governed by SŽ S11 “Prostorová průchodnost tratí” and ČSN 73 6320. This establishes the hierarchy, not the numerical tables. The PDF labels itself SŽDC; do not imply this is a newly revised 2026 SŽ document.
- **ČSN 73 6360-1:2013 + Z1**: [public preview](https://nahledy.normy.biz/n.php?i=511473) says it “stanoví požadavky na geometrické parametry koleje a kolejových spojení a rozvětvení z hlediska návrhu koleje železničních drah normálního rozchodu” (sets requirements for track geometry and connections/turnouts for normal-gauge railway design). Full tables are not available in the preview; numerical values below are not claimed as extracted from the current full standard.
- **SŽ S11 / ČSN 73 6320**: governing spatial-clearance references above. Public [ČSN 73 6320 preview](https://nahledy.normy.biz/nahled.php?i=21663) describes its scope as clearance profiles on standard-gauge national, regional and siding railways. A public SŽ presentation identifies SŽ S11 as effective 1 March 2021 and ČSN 73 6320 Z1; it is secondary evidence: [SUDOP presentation](https://www.sudop.cz/files/Cms:StaticPages:Blocks/17_Vrbata_S%C5%BD.pdf). Numeric profile/track-centre clearances remain **not verified** here. The 4.75 m track-centre spacing found in a project search result is project-specific, not a general norm.
- **TSI INF correction (verified 2026-11-15):** Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) **2026/253 is not the Infrastructure TSI**; it concerns the telematics subsystem (TEL TSI), as its exact EUR-Lex record states: [2026/253 EUR-Lex PDF](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ:L_202600253). The previous claim that it repealed the INF TSI was incorrect and is withdrawn. The official EUR-Lex consolidated text located for the Infrastructure TSI is Commission Regulation (EU) No 1299/2014 consolidated **28 September 2023**: [CELEX:02014R1299-20230928](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02014R1299-20230928). The latest relevant amending act located is Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/1694: [EUR-Lex act record](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2023/1694/oj/eng); ERA’s [Infrastructure TSI page](https://www.era.europa.eu/domains/technical-specifications-interoperability/infrastructure-tsi_en) identifies this 2023 act. The earlier base-act record is [1299/2014](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/1299/oj/eng). No later INF replacement was found in this check. The EUR-Lex HTML endpoint was temporarily unavailable during direct retrieval, so this pass verifies the exact current/consolidated document identity and corrects the false 2026/253 attribution, but has **not** extracted the INF annex numeric tables. Its precise parameters and any subsequent amendments still need implementation-level review. TSI applicability is not universal to every retained track: the regulation’s scope and provisions distinguish new, upgraded or renewed infrastructure from existing infrastructure outside those works. For a relocation, identify the legal project scope and authorisation/TSI regime; do not infer applicability solely from the word “relocation”.

### Cant formula: physics, not a design allowance

For standard gauge, equilibrium cant is approximately `D_eq(mm) = 11.8 × V(km/h)^2 / R(m)`. This is a physical relation (with rounding), not an allowed Czech cant, deficiency, excess, or minimum-radius rule. With mixed traffic, cant selected for one speed causes deficiency for faster traffic and excess for slower traffic; the permissible operational envelope and transition rates require the applicable current norm and vehicle approvals.

## Noise law and relocation

Current consolidated **NV 272/2011 Sb. §12(3), Annex 3 Part A**: [officially searchable Czech text](https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/2011-272). Section 12(3) sets the 50 dB base and refers to Annex 3 Part A for corrections; the night-time rail exception is prose in **Annex 3 Part A**, not in §12(3). For ordinary protected buildings and railways placed and permitted by decision or measure **after 31 December 2000**, Annex 3 Part A correction 2 is +10 dB, giving **60 dB day**; the annex then specifies an additional **−5 dB at night for railway and tramway traffic**, giving **55 dB night**. These are hygienic limits for protected outdoor residential space, not rail-source emission inputs.

Do not generalize those numbers to every existing railway: correction 3 is **+18 dB** for infrastructure placed and permitted **before 1 January 2001**, and also applies to the §2(p) activity described in the regulation. Thus “existing railway = 55/50 dB” is not a valid general statement under this current scheme. A relocation’s applicable correction remains **unresolved**: although it is physically new infrastructure, its legal placement/permit status and any applicable §2(p) rule must be determined for the actual project. Retain 60/55 only for the newly permitted ordinary protected-building case above. For a proposed route replacing an existing rail source, noise accounting must also replace—not double-count—the old operation.

- Public SŽ tender documents commonly include priced “Soupis prací” and quantities; example indexed item-level contract amendment: [SŽ contract amendment, work schedule attachment](https://zakazky.spravazeleznic.cz/document_audit_179944/dodatek-c-1-k-sod-e650-s-5015_2020_21_02_2022_ke-zverejneni-pdf). Search metadata quotes line items for overhead contact-line wire supports, but this is not a complete railway unit-cost basis and the original priced schedule was not extracted/normalised here.
- No defensible current Czech unit prices were verified in this pass for track installation, ballast supply/placement, subgrade/formation, electrification, bridges, culverts or tunnels. Do **not** use road SPK category prices or international per-km averages as substitutes. To price: select recent SŽ procurement with public priced quantities, extract relevant line items and units from the awarded schedule, document year/VAT/scope/region/traffic staging, and separately model earthworks/structures/overhead-line systems. Contracts bundle enabling works, possessions, signalling, drainage and risk; a total divided by kilometres is not an item rate.

## Additional numeric evidence: VUT cant and transitions (older teaching source)

The linked VUT lecture PDF [“Převýšení koleje a vzestupnice”](https://www.fce.vutbr.cz/zel/plasek.o/studium/2_Prevyseni_a_vzestupnice.pdf) was downloaded and text-extracted (raw `/tmp/vut-cant-transition.pdf`, text `/tmp/vut-cant-transition.txt`). It is by Otto Plášek, VUT Faculty of Civil Engineering, and explicitly says it is for student study only. PDF metadata dates its creation to 16 October 2009. It cites **ČSN 73 6360-1** but gives no edition/year on the table pages; do not relabel it as the current norm. The source says it is for study purposes only. The values and equations below are independently expressed in prose; no source figure or table is reproduced. Treat them as **older, secondary/provisional** inputs requiring comparison with current ČSN 73 6360-1/-2 and TSI INF before use.

- PDF page 6 (printed slide “Doporučené převýšení”): `D_N1 = 7.1 V²/R` mm for `V ≤ 120 km/h`; `D_N2 = 6.5 V²/R` for `120 < V ≤ 160`; `D_N3 = 5.9 V²/R` for `160 < V ≤ 200`. This is the teaching slide’s recommended cant formula, distinct from equilibrium cant `D_eq = 11.8 V²/R`.
- PDF page 4 (slide “Základní podmínky”): recommended cant range 20 mm to a traffic-load-dependent limit: **150 mm** outside turnouts at ≤20 million t/year, **120 mm** above that. Maximum cant **160 mm**, only outside turnouts and for `R ≥ 290 m`; for smaller radius, `D ≤ (R−50)/1.5`. Turnout value is below 80 mm limit and 120 mm maximum. These are explicit qualifications; 160 mm is not a general unconditional cap.
- PDF page 9, table “Nedostatek převýšení v běžné koleji” (source’s own categories): standard cant deficiency `I_n=80 mm` through 230 km/h and `60 mm` above 230; limit `I_lim=100 mm` through 250 km/h and `80 mm` above 250; maximum `I_max=100 mm` through 80 km/h (130 mm only subject to footnote c), `130 mm` for 80–230 (150 mm only for passenger-traffic vehicles), `130 mm` for 230–250 (150 mm only subject to both footnotes a and b), and `130 mm` for 250–300. Footnote a restricts use to vehicles with maximum axle load 18 t; footnote b to passenger trains; footnote c imposes an additional radius condition and otherwise restricts use to vehicles with axle load ≤18 t; values above the limit require vehicles approved for the higher deficiency and the higher operating speed must be identified as V130/V150. **Do not take 130 mm as an ordinary mixed passenger/freight allowance**: that table explicitly distinguishes conventional vehicles from approved higher-deficiency use and passenger-only cases.
- PDF page 7: cant excess `E_lim=80 mm`, `E_max=110 mm`; teaching material states track cannot be designed with negative deficiency/excess. These are speed-specific excess/deficiency definitions relative to `D_eq`, not an independent unrestricted operating tolerance.
- PDF page 11: sudden change in cant deficiency table. Main/station through track: `V≤100`: standard ΔI 50 mm, maximum 100; `100<V≤120`: standard 40, limit 85, max 85; `120<V≤170`: standard 40, limit 50, max 60; `170<V≤230`: standard 30, limit 40, max 60 (mm). Other track/junction columns differ (for 120–170: standard 60, limit 80, max 80). So for up-to-160 prototype the through-track row is relevant only if current rules corroborate it; it is not a rate per second and not a replacement for transition design.
- PDF page 14, table “Sklon lineární vzestupnice”: for 80–160 km/h, standard `n=10V`, `dD/dt=27.78 mm/s`; for 80–120 km/h, limit `n=7V`, `39.68 mm/s` and max/min `n=6V`, `46.30 mm/s`; for 120–160 km/h, limit `n=8V`, `34.72 mm/s` and max/min `n=7V`, `39.68 mm/s`. For speeds above 160 through 200 km/h, standard `n=12V`, `23.15 mm/s`. (Here n is the inverse gradient parameter convention shown in the slide; actual cant gradient is `1:n`.) In the ≤80 band, footnote a attaches the 1:445 gradient cap to the **limit-column `6V`**, and footnote b attaches 1:400 to the maximum/minimum column; neither footnote is attached to the standard `10V` column. The M0 helper's `max(10V,445)` is an explicitly **PROVISIONAL conservative policy extension**, avoiding a steeper low-speed ordinary ramp than the limit-column floor. It is not a sourced standard-column minimum. For outer linear transition length, slide gives `L_d = nD/1000 = V D/(3.6(dD/dt))`. It prescribes a linear ramp ordinarily, with Bloss as an alternative where linear cannot be used; see pages 12–13 for equations. These rates are not interchangeable with `dI/dt` limits.
- The 2009 VUT introductory lecture [“Úvod do železničních staveb”](https://www.fce.vutbr.cz/zel/plasek.o/studium/5_zeleznice_uvod.pdf), raw `/tmp/vut-rail-intro.pdf` and `/tmp/vut-rail-intro.txt`, also states PDF p. 27 that the maximum longitudinal grade “nemá přesáhnout 40‰” (should not exceed 40‰), and says the grade depends on starting and braking conditions. **This is not a mixed freight/passenger ruling-gradient standard** and should not become a prototype grade check. It identifies a vertical break as a second-degree parabolic curve (PDF p. 28), but does not give a verified current radius rule here. Elsewhere it illustrates railbed sections and ballast/track dimensions; source is older teaching, not current S4.

## Prototype boundary / follow-up required

A defensible narrow quantity helper can use the explicit no-cant, single-track S3 X section: 3,100 mm formation half-width, 1,700 mm ballast half-width, 1:1.25 ballast side slope, an explicitly supplied applicable SŽ S4 walkway requirement (400 mm is only the constrained-reconstruction exception in clause 11), plus the 350 mm minimum ballast depth for a concrete-sleeper through track. Clause 45 also specifies the shaped top through the inter-sleeper space; a flat top across 3.4 m is only a gross envelope. A quantity helper must accept the selected upper profile, not remove sleeper solid volume from a universally flat trapezoid and call that exact ballast. Apply the stated Y-sleeper width exceptions only when that sleeper type is explicitly selected. Do not apply cant widening; explicitly mark other engineering checks incomplete. Before enabling scored railway validity, retrieve and verify current ČSN 73 6360-1/-2 limits and transition equations, SŽ S4 formation rules, SŽ S11 and ČSN 73 6320 clearance profiles, the INF annex numeric tables from EUR-Lex consolidated 1299/2014/2023/1694 and applicability, and an operationally supported mixed-train gradient/vertical-curve envelope. Obtain comparable priced SŽ tender schedules. No stations or signalling assumptions are supported by this note.
