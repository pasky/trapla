# Lowered minor roads under motorway embankments: depth and drainage

**Scope.** A minor road/track is lowered below an embankment to fit a small concrete road bridge/box. This note distinguishes a sourced engineering requirement from an early-game rule. Catalogue prices are OTSKP April 2026, CZK excl. VAT. Norms are cited by their public reproduction/technical conditions; verify against current licensed standards and the specific project before design.

## 1. How far can the road be lowered?

- **No general maximum lowering depth was found. UNVERIFIED:** I did not locate a clause in the accessible Czech road standards/TP material prescribing a maximum difference between existing and lowered road level under a bridge. ČSN 73 6101/6110/6109 and ČSN 73 6201 impose geometric, drainage and structure design requirements, not a simple blanket “maximum cut” in the material reviewed. A hard universal depth would therefore be invented. **Confidence: medium** (absence from reviewed public material, not proof no project-specific rule exists).
- **What constrains it in practice:** the available length for approach ramps and the permitted longitudinal gradient; groundwater/ground conditions and retaining works; utility conflicts; ability to drain the low point to an acceptable recipient; flood levels/backwater; road class, access and vehicle requirements. TP 83 explicitly makes retention design depend on “výškových poměrech a úrovni hladiny podzemní vody” and says where no suitable recipient exists the water must be pumped (section 6.1; quote below). **Confidence: high** for drainage constraints.
- **Ramp geometry is the usable game constraint, not a fictitious depth limit.** A grade *g* over approach length *L* gives attainable vertical change Δh = g×L. This is arithmetic, not a norm. For illustration, a 2.0 m lowering at a hypothetical 8% grade needs 25 m of ramp per side; **both 8% and 2.0 m here are a game scenario, not a sourced universal allowance**. Do not label this as compliant for every road class.
- **Real-example depth evidence:** publicly indexed searches found no technically complete, comparable motorway/expressway field-road-underpass record stating both the lowering depth and gravity/pumped disposal. A public Černošice planning decision mentions a “hloubka zahloubení podjezdu” and separately a “čerpací stanice” but the extracted result does not establish that these refer to one road-crossing design or state a usable dimension (https://www.mestocernosice.cz/e_download.php?file=/data/editor/1840cs_1.pdf&original=Rozhodnuti%20o%20umisteni%20stavby%20odb%20Berounka%20Karlstejn.pdf). **Not used as a depth benchmark; confidence low.** Do not treat rail/street cuttings as comparable examples without full drawings and drainage details.
- **Practical recommendation:** make lowering depth an outcome of box clearance + cover and approach profile; evaluate groundwater, drainage and retaining-wall feasibility; flag or refuse the design if the player cannot provide a viable outfall/pump. A numeric maximum such as 1.5 m or 2 m is **UNVERIFIED / PROVISIONAL**, not a sourced Czech rule.

## 2. Drainage design basis

Primary source: [TP 83, Odvodnění pozemních komunikací (2008 PDF)](https://pjpk.rsd.cz/data/USR_001_2_8_TP/TP_083_2008_09.pdf).

- **Low-point collection and conveyance:** use a grated/slot channel or side channel to collect runoff at the sag, with an inlet/sump and inspectable pipe route. TP 83 §4.6 says “Rigoly v podjezdech a podél mostních opěr musí být provedeny s dostatečným odstupem od opěr” (maintenance access). It notes slot drains can replace gutters when flow conveyance is inadequate. **High confidence** for principle; it does not prescribe a single universal detail for every box underpass.
- **Gravity outfall:** appropriate only if invert levels, capacity and recipient permit free discharge without unacceptable tailwater/backwater. A lower ditch/watercourse is not sufficient merely because its ground surface is lower; compare pipe inverts and water level. TP 83 §6.1: “Vypouštění nádrže při dostatečném spádu má být řešeno samospádem.” **High confidence** for gravity preference.
- **Pumping / storage:** TP 83 §6.1: “Pokud není možno vypouštět nádrž do vhodného recipientu, je třeba nádrže … vyprazdňovat … pomocí čerpadel.” It also requires assessment of hydraulic overload and normally a safety overflow: “Zpravidla je zapotřebí bezpečnostní přepad”. **High confidence** for the principle. For a road-sag pump system, include sump, pumps, controls/alarm, power, safe overflow/emergency storage and maintenance access. Exact design flow, duty/standby arrangement, tank size and pump price are site-specific and were not verified as universal parameters.
- **Infiltration:** TP 83 §5.2.2 recommends surface/ground infiltration of clean water where suitable (examples include ditches and infiltration pits); it explicitly conditions underground infiltration on suitable geology. The Czech standard [ČSN 75 9010, Vsakovací zařízení srážkových vod](https://csnonline.agentura-cas.cz/) governs infiltration design (full standard not freely accessible here). Do not assume a soakaway works in clay, high groundwater, contaminated runoff or a protected source area. Require site infiltration/groundwater checks and overflow. **Medium confidence** (TP text public; standard’s detailed criteria not extracted).
- **Grades:** TP 83 §4.7 says road ditches should have longitudinal bottom fall **at least 0.5%**, or **0.3% in difficult conditions**, and if less than 0.5% improve flow by lining the bottom. This applies to **ditches, not automatically to buried drainage pipes**. TP 83 reproduces ČSN 73 6201’s **0.5% minimum and 5% maximum** for the *bottom of a culvert* (§13.4); that is not a storm-sewer rule either. **High confidence within those stated scopes.**
- **Pipe gradient / road-surface cover:** **UNVERIFIED:** no universal minimum storm-drain pipe gradient or minimum pipe cover below the sagging road surface was established from the accessible TP 83 text. A **0.5% pipe grade** can be used as a conservative game screening assumption only, explicitly **PROVISIONAL** pending pipe diameter, flow, sediment and local utility/standard checks. Minimum cover depends on loading, pipe material/class, bedding and frost/structure details; do not use a made-up universal cover. For feasibility, derive the required outlet invert from the inlet sump invert, pipe gradient × route length, fittings/drop structures, and required cover; compare against surveyed recipient water level.

## 4. Worked gravity check (game screening only)

For a pipe route of length *L*, a selected assumed fall *i* needs invert drop Δz = *iL*. At the provisional **0.5%** screening grade, **30 m** of pipe consumes **0.15 m** invert drop (arithmetic; both 0.5% and 30 m are model assumptions, not a universal standard). Check outlet invert plus downstream water level, not terrain alone. If the available drop is insufficient, consider a different route/outfall, engineered drop structure, infiltration where verified, or pumped sump. A pump is not an automatic cure for an unacceptable flood-overflow path.

## 5. Recommended game model

- **Maximum lowering:** no sourced universal depth. Let road profile/ramp length and feasibility tests determine it. If a hard game cap is needed, **2.0 m provisional** is a tunable gameplay safeguard only (not norm-backed); label it **UNVERIFIED** and avoid presenting it as Czech engineering practice. Better: allow deeper cuts only with explicit retaining/drainage works and a warning.
- **Drainage decision:** compute required gravity fall from the sump outlet invert to receiving invert at **0.5% provisional pipe grade**; gravity only if recipient/invert is lower by that amount **plus** actual structure/cover and tailwater allowance. No generic fixed “X m lower within Y m” is supported; use the actual route length and level survey. If no suitable gravity discharge exists, use a pump-and-sump solution with safe overflow/storage, or reject the design if no safe discharge is possible.
- **Collection items (sourced OTSKP):** use 2 × 89712 concrete road gully = **23,490.74 CZK** for two inlets; alternatively use 935111 channel at **3,951.71 CZK/m** plus appropriate 897624 inlet at **13,944.21 CZK each**. Use these as catalogue item values, not complete design costs.
- **Gravity pipe (sourced OTSKP):** DN300 plastic 9183A3 = **1,436.48 CZK/m**; DN400 plastic 9183B3 = **2,372.72 CZK/m**. Concrete 9183A1/9183B1 = **2,501.53 / 3,155.35 CZK/m**; reinforced concrete 9183A2/9183B2 = **2,829.93 / 3,617.50 CZK/m**. Add a manhole if required: plastic DN300/400 = **4,652.34 / 6,089.57 CZK each**; concrete DN≤300/400 = **25,920.93 / 27,487.38 CZK each**.
- **Outlet:** OTSKP 89516 = **8,044.73 CZK each** (sourced drainage outlet; not a full designed headwall); 89536 = **8,644.26 CZK each**.
- **Soakaway:** OTSKP 93629 = **2,310.03 CZK each**, but only as the catalogue-defined VL4 infiltration pit item, not a full engineered soakaway. Treat as unsuitable until infiltration/groundwater/quality conditions pass; complete-system cost **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Pump station:** required when gravity is infeasible and a safe recipient exists at pumped discharge. Complete installed 2026 price **UNVERIFIED** in this research; price it as a separate provisional package only after obtaining a real bill of quantities. Do not hide it in the pipe rate.
- **Overall confidence:** high for extracted OTSKP item rates and TP 83’s stated ditch/culvert/drainage principles; medium for using them as game proxies; low/unverified for any universal maximum lowering, storm-pipe grade/cover rule, real-example lowering range, or complete pump/soakaway cost.

### C. Drainage rule cross-check

The existing [TP 83 §6.1](https://pjpk.rsd.cz/data/USR_001_2_8_TP/TP_083_2008_09.pdf) remains the directly relevant Czech guidance: “Pokud není možno vypouštět nádrž do vhodného recipientu, je třeba nádrže … vyprazdňovat … pomocí čerpadel” and “Zpravidla je zapotřebí bezpečnostní přepad.” This supports pumping where no suitable gravity recipient exists and a safety overflow; it does not prescribe a sag-depth limit or price. **Confidence: high** for the quoted principle, no universal depth limit inferred.

**Observed depth and game cap.** Within this new replacement-underpass sample, the only usable structural depth proxy is Studénka’s derived **about 5.47 m from the road surface to the upper face of the railway main member**; this is not a measured railhead depth and no source reports the original-road level precisely enough to claim a measured lowering range or a largest observed lowering. Its **3.7 m** vehicle clearance plus the **0.97 m** railway superstructure and **0.80 m** road-slab depth explain why a replacement underpass can need a roughly five-metre-plus road depression, unlike the **0.65–1.40 m** clearance adjustments in §6. **Recommended game maximum: 6.0 m below original road level, PROVISIONAL** (not a standard or universal engineering limit), confidence **low-to-medium**: it rounds above the single approximately 5.47 m structural-envelope example and allows a plausible full railway-underpass geometry, but evidence is only one design and its original surface was not surveyed in the report. Prefer a profile-based feasibility test; do not present 6 m as a norm. Ramp grades/lengths, groundwater/retaining conditions, utilities, and a safe outfall remain the actual constraints. Every numerical project value in this paragraph is sourced in the Studénka row; the proposed cap itself is an explicitly game-defined choice, not a claimed observed/normative number.

**Pumping is a common, but conditional, solution at sags.** The Studénka project explicitly designs two pumps for underpass runoff that cannot drain by gravity; the Opava design likewise uses a two-pump station for an existing railway underpass. TP 83 §6.1 gives the governing condition rather than saying every sag must be pumped: “Pokud není možno vypouštět nádrž do vhodného recipientu, je třeba nádrže … vyprazdňovat … pomocí čerpadel” and “Zpravidla je zapotřebí bezpečnostní přepad.” Thus pumping is a recurring normal solution where gravity outfall is unavailable, while gravity remains appropriate where levels and recipient permit. **Confidence: high** for this conditional rule; only medium for how prevalent pumping is across all projects (small, selected sample).

**Pump-station price recommendation.** No published itemized station SO or complete installed road-sag station price was verified in the examined project material.
